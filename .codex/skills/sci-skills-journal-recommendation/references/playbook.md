# Manuscript Quality and Journal Recommendation Playbook

## Quality before names

Assess problem importance, novelty against verified literature, evidence-to-claim fit, design and methods, internal and external validation, statistical or interpretive credibility, reproducibility, figures, completeness, language, article type, audience, and scope. Identify blockers and feasible upgrades before choosing a tier.

If the central claim, article type, methods or evidence route, main results, and manuscript completeness cannot be established, return `BLOCKED/NOT_ASSESSED`, request the minimum missing package, and do not name journals. Abstract-only material may support provisional scope clarification but cannot bypass this gate.

## Verification ledger

For every mutable fact record the fact, source page or user-provided report, checked date, status, and conflict. Use ordinary official journal or publisher webpages and current user-provided JCR, CAS, Scopus, institutional, or journal-list records. Never invent impact factor, quartile, CAS zone, indexing, OA model, APC, acceptance rate, review time, or deadline.

## Tiers

- `STRETCH`: plausible fit requiring stronger novelty, validation, breadth, or presentation.
- `TARGET`: current evidence and scope fit after listed repairs.
- `SAFE`: narrower or application-focused fit without implying guaranteed acceptance.

For every candidate give scope and article-type fit, evidence for fit, quality gap, verified or pending metrics, OA and fee status, risks, and submission order. Exclude predatory, discontinued, mismatched, or unverifiable venues. If manuscript quality is too low, return a repair plan and postpone named recommendations.
